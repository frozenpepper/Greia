## 1. Relational Principles (No Stamped Formulas)

Foliage in Greia represents warm, tactile, storybook nature (inspired by classic European picture books, Tove Jansson, and Studio Ghibli nature backgrounds).

### Core Rules:
1. **Species Growth Morphology over Parametric Shapes:**
   - Never assemble canopies from identical overlapping circles or generic ellipse blobs.
   - Build irregular, organic masses matching the target species:
     - **Mature Broadleaf Oak:** Spreading horizontal masses, heavy low center of gravity, deep under-canopy pockets.
     - **Young Birch / Woodland Tree:** Airy, upward-reaching leaf clumps with visible sky windows and light filtering through branches.
     - **Weeping / Willow:** Cascading vertical teardrop masses with soft hanging tips.
     - **Ground Bramble / Shrub:** Arching woody canes supporting alternating leaf clusters with negative space gaps.
2. **Three-Tier Lighting Hierarchy:**
   - **Tier A (Deep Under-Shade):** Dark rich forest green (`#0E3812` / `#16481A`) providing depth and anchoring bough junctions.
   - **Tier B (Midtone Body):** Lush warm emerald/olive body (`#389234` / `#48A63C`) establishing the primary volume.
   - **Tier C (Sunlit Crest Form Lighting):** Soft, diffuse yellow-green daylight tones (`#68BE4E` / `#9AD876`).
3. **Matte Form Shading vs. Specular Inflatable Gloss:**
   - Never use sharp, high-contrast, pale elliptical highlight blobs. Specular highlights make foliage look like inflated vinyl.
   - Use soft, broad diffuse form gradients that follow the overall three-dimensional volume of the canopy masses.
4. **Organic Edge Rhythm:**
   - Break mechanical arcs with subtle, deliberate leaf notches, asymmetrical crest contours, and botanical overlaps.
