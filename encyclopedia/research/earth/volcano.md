## Source set

Primary authority:

- U.S. Geological Survey — *How Do Volcanoes Erupt?*  
  `https://www.usgs.gov/faqs/how-do-volcanoes-erupt?page=1`

Supported concepts used by this pilot:

- rock can melt below Earth's surface and form magma;
- magma is less dense than surrounding solid rock and can rise, collecting in magma chambers;
- magma can move through vents/fissures to the surface;
- magma at the surface is called lava;
- eruptions can be relatively flowing/effusive or explosive;
- magma composition/viscosity and how easily gas escapes strongly affect eruption style;
- explosive eruptions can fragment magma into tephra, including ash-sized particles.

The pilot intentionally avoids frightening hazard detail, eruption prediction, casualty statistics, and named disaster examples. Those are not needed to teach the basic mechanism to a young child.

## Accepted pilot claims

1. **Magma below, lava above.** Molten rock below the surface is called magma; once it erupts onto the surface it is called lava.
2. **Magma can rise.** Magma can rise through the crust and collect in a magma chamber before moving upward through cracks/vents.
3. **Gas and pressure matter.** If gas escapes easily, an eruption may be more flowing; if gas is trapped in thick/sticky magma, pressure can build and an eruption can be more explosive.
4. **Explosive eruptions can make ash/tephra.** Magma can break into fragments ranging from tiny ash particles to larger pieces.

The page is a schematic teaching model. It must not imply that every volcano has the same plumbing geometry, timing, magma composition, or eruption style.

## Child-facing wording contract

### Intro — Tito

**Italian**  
`Dentro un vulcano può esserci roccia fusa chiamata magma. Tocca la camera magmatica e vediamo come può iniziare un'eruzione!`

**English**  
`Inside a volcano there can be melted rock called magma. Touch the magma chamber and let's see how an eruption can begin!`

### Magma and lava — Tito

**Italian**  
`Sotto la superficie si chiama magma. Quando esce sulla superficie, la roccia fusa si chiama lava.`

**English**  
`Below the surface it is called magma. When the melted rock comes out onto the surface, it is called lava.`

### Rising through vents — Tito

**Italian**  
`Il magma può risalire e raccogliersi sotto il vulcano, poi spingersi verso l'alto attraverso crepe e condotti.`

**English**  
`Magma can rise and collect beneath a volcano, then push upward through cracks and vents.`

### Gas and eruption style — Tito

**Italian**  
`Se i gas riescono a uscire facilmente, la lava può scorrere. Se restano intrappolati in un magma molto denso, la pressione può aumentare e l'eruzione può essere esplosiva.`

**English**  
`If gases can escape easily, lava may flow out. If gas stays trapped in very thick magma, pressure can build and the eruption can be explosive.`

### Ash and fragments — Tito

**Italian**  
`In un'eruzione esplosiva il magma può rompersi in tanti frammenti. I pezzetti più piccoli formano la cenere vulcanica.`

**English**  
`In an explosive eruption, magma can break into many fragments. The tiniest pieces form volcanic ash.`

## Interaction-to-claim mapping

- magma-chamber control -> pressure/bubble build-up + rising-magma animation;
- magma/lava hotspot -> clearly highlight below-surface magma and above-surface lava with different labels/colors;
- vent hotspot -> animate a bright magma path upward through the central vent;
- eruption-style control -> cycle a gentle lava-flow state and a bounded explosive state;
- ash hotspot -> reveal a small bounded cloud of dark particles above the crater.

No physics engine is required. The page model owns only a few explicit states and timers.

## Scope guardrails

- Do not say all volcanoes erupt explosively.
- Do not say pressure alone determines eruption style; child wording keeps gas trapping tied to thick/sticky magma.
- Do not imply the schematic chamber/vent shape is a literal universal cross-section.
- Do not add earthquake prediction, volcanic lightning, pyroclastic flows, lahars, famous eruptions or death/injury material to this first page without a separate product/research decision.
