## Accepted claims

### Roots anchor and absorb

Fine roots absorb water and dissolved nutrients/minerals from soil. Larger woody roots help anchor the tree. Real root systems vary enormously; the child illustration is schematic and should not imply every tree has the same root shape or depth.

### Water travels upward

Water taken up by roots is transported through the tree toward leaves. For this age layer, the page may show one upward pathway without introducing detailed xylem anatomy unless a later researched expansion needs it.

### Leaves use light to make sugars

During photosynthesis, leaves use light energy, water and carbon dioxide to produce sugars that support the tree. Oxygen is produced as part of photosynthesis. Trees also respire; avoid the misleading idea that a tree simply "breathes in carbon dioxide and breathes out oxygen" in the same one-way sense as an animal breathing.

### Water also leaves through leaves

Water vapor moves from leaves into the air through transpiration. This is one route by which water returns from land/vegetation to the atmosphere.

### Trees do not all follow one seasonal costume

Many broad-leaved trees in colder temperate regions shed leaves in autumn/winter, while many conifers retain green needles through the year. In other climates, leaf loss may track a dry season rather than winter. The page may cycle illustrative seasonal states, but narration must make clear that tree species and climates differ.

## Scope guardrails

- Do not describe roots as drinking through one straw-like main root; root systems branch and vary.
- Do not say all tree roots extend deeper than the crown or use one universal root geometry.
- Do not teach a simplistic animal-like breathing analogy as the scientific explanation for gas exchange.
- Do not imply every tree is evergreen or every tree loses leaves each autumn; seasonal behavior varies by species and climate.
- Keep photosynthesis schematic: sunlight + water + carbon dioxide -> sugars, with oxygen released.

## Approved narration wording

### `encyclopedia.earth.tree.intro`

**it:** Un albero continua anche sotto terra. Le radici lo tengono ancorato, assorbono acqua e sali minerali, e le foglie usano la luce del Sole per costruire il cibo dell'albero.

**en:** A tree continues below the ground. Its roots anchor it and absorb water and minerals, while its leaves use sunlight to make food for the tree.

### `encyclopedia.earth.tree.roots`

**it:** Le radici hanno tanti rami sottili che assorbono acqua e sostanze minerali dal terreno. Le radici più grandi aiutano anche a tenere l'albero ben fermo.

**en:** Roots have many fine branches that absorb water and minerals from the soil. Larger roots also help hold the tree firmly in place.

### `encyclopedia.earth.tree.water`

**it:** L'acqua assorbita dalle radici viaggia verso l'alto attraverso il tronco e i rami fino alle foglie. Un albero deve trasportare continuamente acqua alle sue parti vive.

**en:** Water absorbed by the roots travels upward through the trunk and branches to the leaves. A tree must keep moving water to its living parts.

### `encyclopedia.earth.tree.photosynthesis`

**it:** Nelle foglie, l'energia della luce aiuta a usare acqua e anidride carbonica per produrre zuccheri. Questo processo si chiama fotosintesi e produce anche ossigeno.

**en:** In the leaves, light energy helps use water and carbon dioxide to make sugars. This process is called photosynthesis, and it also produces oxygen.

### `encyclopedia.earth.tree.transpiration`

**it:** Una parte dell'acqua esce dalle foglie nell'aria come vapore acqueo. Questo processo si chiama traspirazione ed è anche una parte del ciclo dell'acqua.

**en:** Some water leaves the leaves and enters the air as water vapor. This process is called transpiration, and it is also part of the water cycle.

### `encyclopedia.earth.tree.seasons`

**it:** Gli alberi non cambiano tutti allo stesso modo. Molti alberi a foglia larga perdono le foglie nella stagione fredda, mentre molte conifere restano verdi; in altri climi le foglie possono cadere durante la stagione secca.

**en:** Trees do not all change the same way. Many broad-leaved trees lose their leaves in a cold season, while many conifers stay green; in other climates leaves may fall during a dry season.
