## Accepted claims

### Water keeps moving

Water continuously moves among the ocean, land, atmosphere, ice, rivers, lakes and groundwater. The Sun supplies energy for evaporation and gravity helps move precipitation and runoff downhill.

### Evaporation

Sunlight can provide energy that lets liquid water evaporate into invisible water vapor. The child page should show rising symbols/particles as a teaching cue, but narration must not call visible mist or steam itself "water vapor".

### Condensation and clouds

When water vapor cools, it can condense into tiny liquid droplets. Clouds contain tiny condensed water droplets and, depending on conditions, ice crystals. Clouds are not giant bags of gaseous water vapor.

### Precipitation, runoff and infiltration

Cloud droplets/ice particles can grow until precipitation falls. Rain is the most common form of precipitation. Water reaching land may run downhill into streams and oceans or soak into the ground as infiltration; underground water can later move and rejoin surface waters.

## Scope guardrails

- Do not portray the water cycle as one rigid circular conveyor belt; water can take many pathways and remain stored for very different lengths of time.
- Do not say clouds are made only of water vapor.
- Do not say visible steam/mist is invisible water vapor.
- Do not imply all evaporated water immediately becomes rain.
- Keep the page focused on a familiar rain pathway while explicitly showing both runoff and infiltration after rain.

## Interaction → fact mapping

| interaction | factual purpose |
|---|---|
| warm surface / Sun | evaporation moves liquid water into atmospheric water vapor |
| cool/build cloud | condensation creates tiny droplets/ice particles |
| fill cloud | droplets grow and precipitation can fall |
| ground split | rain can run downhill or infiltrate into the ground |

## Approved narration wording

### `encyclopedia.earth.rain.intro`

**it:** L'acqua sulla Terra è sempre in viaggio. Il Sole può far evaporare l'acqua, in alto si formano le nuvole e poi l'acqua può tornare giù come pioggia.

**en:** Water on Earth is always on the move. The Sun can make water evaporate, clouds can form above, and water can come back down as rain.

### `encyclopedia.earth.rain.evaporation`

**it:** L'energia del Sole può trasformare una parte dell'acqua liquida in vapore acqueo invisibile che sale nell'atmosfera. Questo processo si chiama evaporazione.

**en:** Energy from the Sun can turn some liquid water into invisible water vapor that moves into the atmosphere. This process is called evaporation.

### `encyclopedia.earth.rain.condensation`

**it:** Quando il vapore acqueo si raffredda può condensare in minuscole goccioline. Le nuvole sono formate da tantissime piccole gocce d'acqua e, quando fa abbastanza freddo, anche da cristalli di ghiaccio.

**en:** When water vapor cools, it can condense into tiny droplets. Clouds are made of huge numbers of tiny water droplets and, when it is cold enough, ice crystals too.

### `encyclopedia.earth.rain.precipitation`

**it:** Nelle nuvole le goccioline possono unirsi e crescere. Quando diventano abbastanza grandi e pesanti, l'acqua cade verso terra: la pioggia è una forma di precipitazione.

**en:** Inside clouds, droplets can join and grow. When they become large and heavy enough, water falls toward the ground. Rain is one form of precipitation.

### `encyclopedia.earth.rain.ground`

**it:** Dopo la pioggia, una parte dell'acqua scorre in discesa verso ruscelli, fiumi e oceano. Un'altra parte può infiltrarsi nel terreno e continuare il suo viaggio sottoterra.

**en:** After rain, some water runs downhill toward streams, rivers, and the ocean. Some can soak into the ground and continue its journey underground.
