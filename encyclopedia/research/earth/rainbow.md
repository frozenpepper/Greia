## Accepted claims

### Sunlight and airborne water droplets are both required

A familiar primary rainbow becomes visible when sunlight interacts with water droplets in the air and the viewer is positioned appropriately. For a typical rainbow seen from the ground, the Sun is behind the viewer and droplets are in front.

### Light bends, reflects and bends again

When sunlight enters a droplet it changes direction through refraction. Some light reflects from the inside of the droplet and bends again when leaving it.

### Colors separate because wavelengths bend differently

White sunlight contains many visible wavelengths/colors. Different wavelengths are refracted by slightly different amounts, so the returning light is spread into colors. In a primary rainbow red is on the outer/top side and violet toward the inner/bottom side.

### A rainbow is an optical phenomenon, not an object at one location

The visible bow depends on the positions of the Sun, droplets and observer. A full primary rainbow is geometrically a circle, though from the ground the horizon usually hides part of it and we see an arc.

## Scope guardrails

- Do not teach that water droplets contain stored rainbow colors.
- Do not portray the rainbow as a physical arch the child could walk to or touch at one fixed place.
- Do not claim every rainy sky produces a visible rainbow; geometry and sunlight must be suitable.
- The page uses one enlarged schematic droplet to explain many droplets in the real atmosphere.
- Keep exact angular geometry out of the v0.1 narration; the child interaction only teaches the Sun/viewer/droplet relationship.

## Interaction → fact mapping

| interaction | factual purpose |
|---|---|
| align Sun behind viewer and droplets ahead | suitable viewing geometry makes the rainbow appear |
| enlarged droplet ray path | light refracts entering, reflects inside, refracts leaving |
| split white ray into colors | wavelengths bend by different amounts |
| lift viewpoint / reveal circle | rainbow geometry is circular though ground often shows only an arc |

## Approved narration wording

### `encyclopedia.earth.rainbow.intro`

**it:** Un arcobaleno appare quando la luce del Sole incontra tante goccioline d'acqua nell'aria e noi le guardiamo dalla posizione giusta. Proviamo a mettere Sole, pioggia e osservatore al posto giusto!

**en:** A rainbow appears when sunlight meets many water droplets in the air and we look from the right position. Let's place the Sun, rain, and observer in the right arrangement!

### `encyclopedia.earth.rainbow.position`

**it:** Per vedere un normale arcobaleno, il Sole deve stare dietro di noi e le goccioline d'acqua davanti. L'arcobaleno che vediamo dipende anche dalla posizione di chi guarda.

**en:** To see a typical rainbow, the Sun needs to be behind us and water droplets in front. The rainbow we see also depends on where the observer is standing.

### `encyclopedia.earth.rainbow.droplet`

**it:** Quando la luce entra in una gocciolina cambia direzione. Poi una parte si riflette all'interno e la luce si piega di nuovo quando esce.

**en:** When light enters a water droplet, it changes direction. Some of it then reflects inside, and the light bends again as it leaves.

### `encyclopedia.earth.rainbow.colors`

**it:** La luce bianca del Sole contiene tanti colori. Nella gocciolina i diversi colori si piegano in modo leggermente diverso e così arrivano ai nostri occhi separati.

**en:** White sunlight contains many colors. Inside the droplet, different colors bend by slightly different amounts, so they reach our eyes separated.

### `encyclopedia.earth.rainbow.circle`

**it:** Un arcobaleno completo forma in realtà un cerchio. Da terra, però, il terreno e l'orizzonte di solito ne nascondono una parte e noi vediamo soprattutto un arco.

**en:** A complete rainbow is actually a circle. From the ground, though, the land and horizon usually hide part of it, so we mostly see an arc.
