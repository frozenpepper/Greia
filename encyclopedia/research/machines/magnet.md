## Accepted claims

### Magnets have two poles

A simple permanent magnet has a north-seeking and a south-seeking pole. Opposite poles attract; like poles repel. The force acts through the magnetic field around the magnet rather than because the magnets touch.

### Magnets do not attract every metal

Iron, nickel, cobalt and many alloys containing them respond strongly to ordinary magnets. Many familiar metals, including aluminum and copper, do not respond strongly to a small permanent magnet in the same way. The child page should include both magnetic and non-magnetic-looking metal samples so “metal = magnetic” is never taught.

### Magnetic response depends on material

Ferromagnetic materials can respond strongly because magnetic domains can align. The page does not need electron/domain physics in the spoken layer, but the research record preserves the reason the material test is not arbitrary.

### Attraction and repulsion can move objects without contact

Opposite poles can pull and like poles can push while separated by a small gap. This is a good direct-manipulation interaction because the visible motion is the fact.

## Scope guardrails

- Do not say magnets attract all metals.
- Do not say north is “positive” and south is “negative”; magnetic poles are not electric charge signs.
- Do not imply one pole can be isolated on an ordinary bar magnet.
- Do not claim a magnetic field is a visible substance; field lines in the page are schematic indicators.
- Keep powerful-magnet hazards out of child interaction; this is a virtual demonstration only.

## Interaction → fact mapping

| interaction | factual purpose |
|---|---|
| flip one bar magnet | opposite poles attract; like poles repel |
| move magnets together | force can act across a gap |
| test material samples | some materials respond strongly while many metals do not |
| field-line reveal | magnetic field extends around the magnet; lines are schematic |

## Approved narration wording

### `encyclopedia.machines.magnet.intro`

**it:** Magnete rilevato! Ogni calamita ha due poli. Poli diversi si attirano, mentre poli uguali si respingono anche senza toccarsi.

**en:** Magnet detected! Every magnet has two poles. Different poles attract, while matching poles repel even without touching.

### `encyclopedia.machines.magnet.poles`

**it:** Se avvicini un polo nord a un polo sud, i magneti si attirano. Nord contro nord, oppure sud contro sud, invece si respingono.

**en:** Bring a north pole near a south pole and the magnets attract. North facing north, or south facing south, makes them repel instead.

### `encyclopedia.machines.magnet.materials`

**it:** Attenzione: non tutto il metallo è attirato da una calamita. Ferro, nichel, cobalto e molti materiali che li contengono reagiscono fortemente; alluminio e rame, per esempio, no.

**en:** Careful: a magnet does not attract every metal. Iron, nickel, cobalt, and many materials containing them respond strongly; aluminum and copper, for example, do not.

### `encyclopedia.machines.magnet.distance`

**it:** Due magneti possono tirarsi o spingersi anche con un piccolo spazio in mezzo. La forza magnetica agisce attraverso il campo che circonda il magnete.

**en:** Two magnets can pull or push each other even with a small gap between them. Magnetic force acts through the field around the magnet.

### `encyclopedia.machines.magnet.field`

**it:** Il campo magnetico non si vede direttamente. Le linee che disegniamo intorno alla calamita sono un modo utile per mostrare dove e come può agire la forza.

**en:** A magnetic field cannot be seen directly. The lines we draw around a magnet are a useful way to show where and how its force can act.
