## Accepted claims

### A bridge must carry loads to supports

The bridge deck carries its own weight and temporary loads such as people or vehicles. Structural members transfer those loads through the bridge toward supports/abutments and ultimately the ground. The child page should make this path visible rather than simply showing a bridge bend.

### Members can be in tension or compression

Structural members may be pulled in tension or pushed in compression. In a simplified truss, some members mainly carry one or the other. The exact force pattern depends on geometry, supports and load location, so the page must label its diagrams as schematic rather than universal.

### Shape changes how forces travel

Beam, arch and truss forms carry loads differently. An arch directs substantial compression toward its supports; a truss uses connected members so loads can travel through a network of tension/compression paths. A simple beam bends under load and relies on its section/supports to resist that bending.

### Redundant paths can matter

Real bridge engineering uses multiple load paths and carefully designed connections so forces can be redistributed. This is too advanced for the main narration, but it supports the child concept that a bridge is a connected system rather than one magic strong piece.

## Scope guardrails

- Do not imply one bridge type is always strongest; appropriate form depends on span, material, site and loads.
- Do not use exaggerated deformation as if it were realistic scale. Label it visually as a teaching exaggeration.
- Do not claim every truss member always has the same tension/compression role; load position and design matter.
- Do not present a bridge as carrying loads only downward; supports also provide reactions and members redirect forces.
- No structural-design formulas or real-world load limits in this child page.

## Approved narration wording

### `encyclopedia.machines.bridge.intro`

**it:** Ponte pronto! Un ponte non deve solo stare fermo: deve prendere il peso del ponte stesso e di ciò che passa sopra, poi portare quelle forze fino ai sostegni e al terreno.

**en:** Bridge ready! A bridge does more than stay still. It must carry its own weight and whatever travels across it, then transfer those forces to its supports and the ground.

### `encyclopedia.machines.bridge.load`

**it:** Quando metti un peso sul ponte, la forza entra attraverso l'impalcato. Le parti collegate del ponte la trasferiscono verso i sostegni ai lati.

**en:** When you place a load on a bridge, the force enters through the deck. Connected parts of the bridge transfer it toward the supports at the sides.

### `encyclopedia.machines.bridge.shapes`

**it:** Una trave, un arco e una capriata non portano il peso nello stesso modo. La forma cambia il percorso delle forze attraverso la struttura.

**en:** A beam, an arch, and a truss do not carry a load in the same way. The shape changes the path forces take through the structure.

### `encyclopedia.machines.bridge.forces`

**it:** Alcune parti di una struttura vengono compresse, cioè spinte insieme. Altre sono in tensione, cioè vengono tirate. In un ponte vero il disegno decide dove agiscono queste forze.

**en:** Some parts of a structure are in compression, meaning they are pushed together. Others are in tension, meaning they are pulled. In a real bridge, the design determines where those forces act.

### `encyclopedia.machines.bridge.path`

**it:** Segui le frecce: il carico passa da una parte collegata alla successiva fino ai sostegni. Un ponte funziona come un sistema di percorsi per le forze.

**en:** Follow the arrows: the load passes through connected parts until it reaches the supports. A bridge works as a system of paths for forces.
