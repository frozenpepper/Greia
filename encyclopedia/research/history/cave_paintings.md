## Accepted claims

### People made images on cave and rock surfaces long ago

Ancient people created paintings, hand stencils/prints, geometric marks and images of animals on cave and rock-shelter surfaces in many parts of the world. Some known examples are tens of thousands of years old. One Sulawesi animal painting has been dated to at least about 45,000 years ago; Cueva de las Manos contains many hand outlines dating roughly 13,000–9,500 years ago.

### Natural pigments were used

Ancient artists used mineral pigments and other naturally available materials. Different sites used different methods and recipes. The v0.1 child interaction may simulate ochre-like pigment marks, but should not claim one universal paint recipe.

### We can see what was depicted without always knowing why

Animals, hands, human figures, signs and geometric forms appear in ancient art. Archaeologists can study pigments, locations, dates and subject matter, but the exact meaning or purpose of many images remains uncertain. The page must distinguish observation from interpretation.

### Preservation depends on place and conditions

Some art survived because protected cave/rock-shelter locations limited weathering and disturbance. Survival is selective; what remains is not a complete record of everything ancient people made.

## Scope guardrails

- Never invent a specific ritual, story or message as fact when the meaning is unknown.
- Do not imply all prehistoric people painted caves or that all cave art comes from Europe.
- Do not call one example "the first art"; discoveries and dating can change.
- Do not let the child permanently modify source artwork. Any mark-making interaction is a temporary Activity overlay/resettable sandbox.
- Do not depict ancient people as unintelligent or primitive caricatures.
- Avoid presenting one pigment recipe as universal.

## Interaction → fact mapping

| interaction | factual purpose |
|---|---|
| move a lamp across the wall | protected cave surfaces preserve visual evidence and changing light reveals forms |
| reveal hands/animals/geometric marks | ancient imagery included several kinds of subjects |
| pigment reveal | mineral/natural pigments could be used to create color |
| temporary child mark | making a mark demonstrates technique without pretending we know the ancient artist's intended meaning |

## Approved narration wording

### `encyclopedia.history.cave_paintings.intro`

**it:** Moltissimo tempo fa, persone in tante parti del mondo crearono immagini su pareti di grotte e ripari rocciosi. Alcune sono sopravvissute per decine di migliaia di anni.

**en:** A very long time ago, people in many parts of the world made images on cave walls and rock shelters. Some have survived for tens of thousands of years.

### `encyclopedia.history.cave_paintings.images`

**it:** Sulle pareti possiamo trovare animali, mani, figure umane e forme geometriche. Possiamo osservare ciò che è stato dipinto, ma spesso non sappiamo con certezza che significato avesse per chi lo creò.

**en:** On these walls we can find animals, hands, human figures, and geometric shapes. We can observe what was painted, but we often do not know exactly what it meant to the people who made it.

### `encyclopedia.history.cave_paintings.pigments`

**it:** Gli artisti antichi potevano ottenere colori da materiali naturali, compresi pigmenti minerali. Luoghi e tempi diversi usavano materiali e tecniche diverse.

**en:** Ancient artists could make colors from natural materials, including mineral pigments. Different places and times used different materials and techniques.

### `encyclopedia.history.cave_paintings.hands`

**it:** In alcuni siti ci sono tantissime sagome di mani. Per crearle, una mano poteva essere appoggiata alla parete mentre il pigmento veniva applicato intorno, lasciando il suo contorno.

**en:** Some sites contain many hand stencils. One way to make them was to hold a hand against the wall while pigment was applied around it, leaving the hand's outline.

### `encyclopedia.history.cave_paintings.uncertainty`

**it:** Gli archeologi raccolgono indizi su età, pigmenti, immagini e posizione, ma per molte pitture il significato preciso resta sconosciuto. Dire «non lo sappiamo ancora» è una parte importante della scienza.

**en:** Archaeologists gather clues about age, pigments, images, and location, but the exact meaning of many paintings remains unknown. Saying “we don't know yet” is an important part of science.
