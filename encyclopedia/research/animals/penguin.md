# Penguin — factual authoring record

**Page ID:** `animals.penguin`  
**Research status:** READY FOR AUTHORING

**Purpose:** provenance and child-safe wording authority for the Greia Encyclopedia generic penguin entry.

This is a generic penguin page. It deliberately avoids the common false shortcut that every penguin lives on Antarctic ice. Species range from Antarctica and sub-Antarctic islands to temperate South America, southern Africa, Australia/New Zealand and the Galápagos region.

## Source set

### Australian Antarctic Program — Penguins

- `https://www.antarctica.gov.au/about-antarctica/animals/penguins/`

Supported concepts used here:

- penguins are flightless birds highly adapted to the marine environment;
- flippers propel them underwater while feet and tail act as rudders;
- their short overlapping feathers form a waterproof coat;
- a layer of body fat contributes to insulation;
- penguins occur only in the Southern Hemisphere, with the greatest concentrations around Antarctic/sub-Antarctic coasts and islands.

### Smithsonian Ocean — Penguins

- `https://ocean.si.edu/ocean-life/seabirds/penguins`

Supported/corroborating concepts:

- penguin wings function as powerful swimming propellers;
- legs/feet placed toward the rear contribute to the characteristic land posture and work as streamlined rudders underwater;
- penguin species occupy very different climates, including Antarctic ice, temperate forests/coasts and warm/equatorial environments;
- feathers are important for insulation and water protection;
- most penguin species gather in breeding colonies, but nest/egg-care strategies vary considerably;
- emperor and king penguins are notable examples that do not build a conventional nest and instead incubate an egg on the feet.

## Accepted claims

1. **A bird built for swimming.** Penguins are flightless birds. Their flippers propel them underwater, while feet and tail help steer.
2. **Protective feathers.** Short overlapping feathers help form a water-resistant coat, and feathers plus body fat help insulate the bird.
3. **Not all ice.** Penguins are Southern Hemisphere birds, but different species live in climates ranging from Antarctic ice to temperate and even warm regions.
4. **Breeding varies by species.** Most species gather in breeding colonies, but nests and egg care differ. Some build nests; emperor and king penguins carry the egg on their feet instead.

## Child-facing wording contract

### Intro — Tito

**Italian**  
`Il pinguino è un uccello che non vola nell'aria: le sue ali sono diventate pinne fortissime per nuotare. Tocca i simboli e tuffiamoci!`

**English**  
`A penguin is a bird that does not fly through the air: its wings have become powerful flippers for swimming. Touch the symbols and let's dive in!`

### Swimming — Tito

**Italian**  
`Sott'acqua le pinne spingono il pinguino in avanti, mentre piedi e coda aiutano a cambiare direzione.`

**English**  
`Underwater, a penguin's flippers push it forward while its feet and tail help it steer.`

### Feathers and insulation — Tito

**Italian**  
`Il pinguino ha tante penne corte e sovrapposte che aiutano a tenere fuori l'acqua. Le penne e uno strato di grasso aiutano anche a trattenere il calore.`

**English**  
`A penguin has many short overlapping feathers that help keep water out. Feathers and a layer of body fat also help hold in heat.`

### Where penguins live — Tito

**Italian**  
`Non tutti i pinguini vivono sul ghiaccio! Vivono nell'emisfero sud, ma alcune specie abitano zone temperate o perfino molto calde.`

**English**  
`Not every penguin lives on ice! Penguins live in the Southern Hemisphere, but some species live in temperate or even very warm places.`

### Breeding groups — Tito

**Italian**  
`Molte specie si riuniscono in colonie per riprodursi, ma non fanno tutte lo stesso nido. I pinguini imperatore e reale tengono perfino l'uovo sopra i piedi.`

**English**  
`Many penguin species gather in colonies to breed, but they do not all make the same kind of nest. Emperor and king penguins even keep the egg on top of their feet.`

## Interaction-to-claim mapping

- swim hotspot -> toggle a land/water scene; underwater the same penguin uses flipper strokes and a gentle steering arc instead of a faster-but-unsourced speed claim;
- feathers hotspot -> reveal a small magnified overlapping-feather layer plus a simple insulation band;
- world hotspot -> switch a schematic globe/landscape among Antarctic ice, temperate coast and warm rocky coast so the child can see that `penguin != ice only`;
- breeding hotspot -> reveal a bounded group of penguins and alternate between a simple nest example and an emperor-style egg-on-feet example.

## Scope guardrails

- Do not say all penguins live in Antarctica or on ice.
- Do not use one species' dive depth, speed, chick care or colony size as a generic penguin fact.
- Do not imply all species build nests; emperor/king penguins are explicit counterexamples.
- Do not imply feathers alone provide all insulation; body fat also contributes.
- Keep swimming mathematical and schematic; no water/physics simulation.
