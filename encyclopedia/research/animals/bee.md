# Bee — factual authoring record

**Page ID:** `animals.bee`  
**Research status:** READY FOR AUTHORING  
**Reviewed:** 2026-08-27  
**Purpose:** provenance and child-safe wording authority for the Greia Encyclopedia generic bee entry.

This page deliberately corrects a common simplification: **not all bees live in hives or colonies**. The generic entry teaches flowers, nectar, pollen and pollination, then contrasts solitary wild bees with the familiar social honey bee case.

## Source set

### USDA Forest Service — Bee nourished / pollen nutrition

- `https://research.fs.usda.gov/treesearch/69291`
- `https://research.fs.usda.gov/rmrs/articles/pollen-nutrition-structures-bee-and-plant-community-interactions`

Supported concepts:

- bees forage on flowers for nectar and pollen;
- pollen is an important source of protein/fats for bees;
- nectar provides sugar-rich energy;
- both wild bees and honey bees collect pollen.

### USDA Forest Service — Pollinator Habitat in Log Landings: Wild Bees

- `https://research.fs.usda.gov/nrs/understory/pollinator-habitat-log-landings-wild-bees`

Supported concepts:

- the vast majority of wild bees are solitary;
- many wild bees nest in the ground, while others use woody debris or hollow stems;
- wild bees are important pollinators.

### USDA / pollinator education material

- `https://www.usda.gov/sites/default/files/documents/pollinator-week-factsheet-06.25.2020.pdf`
- `https://www.fs.usda.gov/Internet/FSE_DOCUMENTS/fseprd548063.pdf`

Supporting concepts:

- honey bees live in colonies with a queen, female workers and male drones;
- worker honey bees collect nectar and pollen;
- bees can transfer pollen while moving among flowers, helping plants reproduce.

## Accepted claims

1. **Flowers provide food.** Bees visit flowers for sugar-rich nectar and nutrient-rich pollen.
2. **Pollen travels.** Pollen can stick to a bee's body and be carried to another flower; this can pollinate the plant.
3. **Pollination helps plants reproduce.** Moving compatible pollen between flowers allows many flowering plants to make seeds and/or fruit.
4. **Bee homes differ.** Most wild bees are solitary rather than hive-living. Honey bees are a familiar social exception that live in large colonies.

## Child-facing wording contract

### Intro — Tito

**Italian**  
`Guarda l'ape tra i fiori! Cerca nettare e polline e, volando da un fiore all'altro, può aiutare le piante a riprodursi.`

**English**  
`Look at the bee among the flowers! It searches for nectar and pollen and, by flying from flower to flower, can help plants reproduce.`

### Nectar and pollen — Tito

**Italian**  
`I fiori offrono due cibi importanti: il nettare è ricco di zuccheri, mentre il polline contiene sostanze nutritive importanti per le api.`

**English**  
`Flowers offer two important foods: nectar is rich in sugars, while pollen contains important nutrients for bees.`

### Carrying pollen — Tito

**Italian**  
`Quando un'ape visita un fiore, un po' di polline può restare sul suo corpo. Sul fiore successivo, parte di quel polline può essere trasferita.`

**English**  
`When a bee visits a flower, some pollen can stick to its body. At the next flower, some of that pollen can be transferred.`

### Pollination — Tito

**Italian**  
`Questo trasporto di polline si chiama impollinazione. Per molte piante è un passaggio importante per formare nuovi semi e frutti.`

**English**  
`This movement of pollen is called pollination. For many plants it is an important step in making new seeds and fruits.`

### Different homes — Tito

**Italian**  
`Non tutte le api vivono in un alveare. La maggior parte delle api selvatiche vive da sola; le api da miele, invece, vivono in grandi colonie.`

**English**  
`Not all bees live in a hive. Most wild bees live alone, while honey bees live together in large colonies.`

## Interaction-to-claim mapping

- nectar/pollen hotspot -> reveal a nectar drop and pollen grains on the current flower;
- pollen hotspot -> move the bee to the next flower while a bounded set of pollen dots visibly rides on its body;
- pollination hotspot -> transferred pollen lights the second flower and a simple seed/fruit icon appears;
- homes hotspot -> toggle between one solitary ground/stem nest and a clearly labelled-as-honey-bee social hive visual.

The page should make the pollen transfer visible enough to understand with speakers muted.

## Scope guardrails

- Never say all bees live in hives or colonies.
- Never imply honey bees are the only or universally best pollinators.
- Do not claim every flower needs a bee; wind and other animals also pollinate plants.
- Do not teach honey production as the defining purpose of bees.
- Avoid fear-based sting messaging in the first page.
