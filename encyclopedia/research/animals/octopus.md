## Source set

### NOAA Fisheries — Octopus / stock assessment material

- `https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/tags/octopus`
- `https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/resource/data/2018-assessment-octopus-stock-complex-bering-sea-and-aleutian-islands`

Useful supported claim:

- octopuses (order Octopoda) have eight appendages/arms; unlike squid-style cephalopod anatomy, they do not have tentacles.

Related NOAA Kodiak Lab educational material also describes suckers running along octopus arms and their touch/sensory role:

- `https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/science-blog/view-inside-kodiak-lab-aquarium-post-4`

### Smithsonian Ocean — camouflage

- `https://ocean.si.edu/ocean-life/invertebrates/how-octopuses-and-squids-change-color`

Useful supported claims:

- octopuses can rapidly change skin color/pattern;
- many thousands of pigment cells called chromatophores contribute to those changes;
- camouflage helps hide from predators/backgrounds;
- octopuses can also spray ink as a defense/escape aid.

### Monterey Bay Aquarium — giant Pacific octopus

- `https://www.montereybayaquarium.org/animals-the-ocean/animals-a-to-z/giant-pacific-octopus`

Useful supporting claims/examples:

- sophisticated camouflage involves pigment cells, muscle fibers and nerves;
- a cloud of ink can form a temporary visual barrier while an octopus escapes.

Species-specific details from this source must not be generalized to all octopuses unless independently supported.

### Smithsonian Ocean — cephalopod circulatory system

- `https://ocean.si.edu/ocean-life/invertebrates/octopuses-squids-and-relatives`

Useful supported claim:

- cephalopods, including octopuses, have three hearts: two branchial hearts pump blood toward the gills, while the systemic heart pumps oxygenated blood around the body.

## Accepted pilot claims

Use only these claims in the first page unless this record is deliberately extended with provenance.

1. **Eight arms, not tentacles.** Octopuses have eight arms; suckers run along the arms.
2. **Camouflage.** Many octopuses can change skin color/pattern rapidly; chromatophores are central to this ability and it can help them blend into surroundings.
3. **Ink defense.** Many octopuses can release ink as part of an escape/defense response.
4. **Three hearts.** An octopus has three hearts; two serve the gills and one pumps blood around the rest of the body.

## Child-facing wording contract

The following is the research-approved semantic wording for voice production. Future wording changes must use the voice-queue revision protocol once recording begins.

### Intro — Tito

**Italian**  
`Guarda che polpo! Ha otto braccia morbide, piene di ventose. Tocca il polpo e scopri i suoi trucchi!`

**English**  
`Look at this octopus! It has eight soft arms covered in suckers. Touch it and discover its tricks!`

### Arms — Tito

**Italian**  
`Un polpo ha otto braccia, non tentacoli. Le ventose lungo le braccia lo aiutano a toccare e afferrare.`

**English**  
`An octopus has eight arms, not tentacles. Suckers along its arms help it touch and hold things.`

### Camouflage — Tito

**Italian**  
`Molti polpi possono cambiare molto in fretta il colore e il disegno della pelle. Così possono confondersi con quello che hanno intorno.`

**English**  
`Many octopuses can change the color and pattern of their skin very quickly. That helps them blend into their surroundings.`

### Ink — Tito

**Italian**  
`Se c'è pericolo, molti polpi possono spruzzare una nuvola d'inchiostro e scappare mentre il predatore è confuso.`

**English**  
`When danger comes, many octopuses can release a cloud of ink and escape while the predator is confused.`

### Three hearts — Tito

**Italian**  
`Sorpresa: un polpo ha tre cuori! Due spingono il sangue verso le branchie e uno lo manda nel resto del corpo.`

**English**  
`Surprise: an octopus has three hearts! Two send blood to the gills, and one sends it around the rest of the body.`
