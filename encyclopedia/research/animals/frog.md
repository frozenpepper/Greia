## Source set

### American Museum of Natural History — Frogs: A Chorus of Colors

- Tadpoles: `https://www.amnh.org/exhibitions/frogs-a-chorus-of-colors/reproduction/tadpoles`
- Reproduction: `https://www.amnh.org/exhibitions/frogs-a-chorus-of-colors/reproduction`
- Nests and Nurseries: `https://www.amnh.org/exhibitions/frogs-a-chorus-of-colors/reproduction/nests-and-nurseries`

Supported concepts used here:

- most frogs/amphibians have a larval tadpole stage followed by metamorphosis into a froglet/adult;
- the classic eggs-in-water -> swimming tadpole -> frog sequence is familiar but not universal;
- AMNH notes that only about half of frog species reproduce through that familiar aquatic pattern and documents many alternative moist nesting strategies.

### U.S. National Park Service — amphibian life cycles

- `https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/cascades-frog.htm`
- `https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/nrca_glca_2021_amphibians.htm`
- `https://www.nps.gov/articles/reptiles-and-amphibians-distribution.htm`

Supported/corroborating concepts:

- frogs are amphibians with larval and adult stages that can look very different;
- in the familiar aquatic cycle tadpoles use a tail and gills, then grow legs, develop lungs and reabsorb the tail during metamorphosis;
- amphibians have permeable/glandular skin and many species depend strongly on aquatic or moist habitats.

### Smithsonian National Zoo — amphibian skin

- `https://nationalzoo.si.edu/animals/news/happy-amphibian-week-2025`

Supporting concept:

- amphibian skin is moist/permeable and contributes importantly to gas exchange; some amphibians lack lungs entirely.

## Accepted claims

1. **Frogs are amphibians.** Their life history often connects aquatic/moist and land stages, although species vary greatly.
2. **A common frog cycle.** A familiar pattern is egg -> tadpole -> froglet -> adult frog.
3. **Metamorphosis changes the body.** In that common pattern, a tadpole grows legs and lungs while the tail becomes smaller as it transforms into a froglet.
4. **Skin matters.** Frog/amphibian skin is moist and permeable and participates in gas exchange, so moisture is biologically important.
5. **Not one universal recipe.** Frog reproduction is unusually diverse; many species use eggs/tadpoles in water, while others use different moist nests or development strategies.

## Child-facing wording contract

### Intro — Tito

**Italian**  
`Le rane sono anfibi e durante la crescita possono cambiare tantissimo. Segui una trasformazione molto comune: dall'uovo al girino, poi alla piccola rana!`

**English**  
`Frogs are amphibians and can change enormously as they grow. Follow one very common transformation: from egg to tadpole and then to a little frog!`

### Common life cycle — Tito

**Italian**  
`In un ciclo molto comune, dall'uovo nasce un girino che vive nell'acqua. Crescendo diventa una ranocchietta e poi una rana adulta.`

**English**  
`In one very common life cycle, an egg hatches into a tadpole that lives in water. As it grows, it becomes a froglet and then an adult frog.`

### Metamorphosis — Tito

**Italian**  
`Questa grande trasformazione si chiama metamorfosi. Nel ciclo classico il girino sviluppa le zampe e i polmoni, mentre la coda diventa sempre più piccola.`

**English**  
`This big transformation is called metamorphosis. In the classic cycle, the tadpole grows legs and lungs while its tail becomes smaller and smaller.`

### Moist skin — Tito

**Italian**  
`La pelle delle rane è umida e permeabile e partecipa anche alla respirazione. Per questo l'acqua e l'umidità sono molto importanti per molti anfibi.`

**English**  
`Frog skin is moist and permeable and also helps with breathing. That is why water and moisture are very important for many amphibians.`

### Many different frog families — Tito

**Italian**  
`Attenzione: non tutte le rane crescono nello stesso modo! Molte usano acqua e girini, ma altre depongono le uova in nidi umidi molto diversi.`

**English**  
`But not every frog grows in exactly the same way! Many use water and tadpoles, while others lay eggs in very different moist nurseries.`

## Interaction-to-claim mapping

- cycle hotspot -> advance a finite egg -> tadpole -> froglet -> adult sequence;
- metamorphosis hotspot -> animate the common tadpole model growing hind/front legs while its tail shortens;
- skin/moisture hotspot -> add a bounded rain/water sheen and simple breathing arrows at the skin surface;
- variety hotspot -> toggle from the classic pond scene to two clearly stylized alternative moist nursery examples (leaf/foam), labelled as `different species`, not stages of the same frog.

## Scope guardrails

- Never say every frog lays eggs directly in open water or every species has the same tadpole sequence; AMNH documents extensive variation.
- Do not give one species' metamorphosis duration as a universal frog timeline.
- Do not say adult frogs breathe only with lungs; amphibian skin also participates in gas exchange and species differ.
- Do not teach `frog = always lives beside a pond`; adult habitat varies widely.
- Keep alternative reproductive strategies schematic and gentle; no disturbing parental-care examples are needed for v0.1.
