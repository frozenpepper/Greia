## Accepted claims

### The largest planet

Jupiter is the fifth planet from the Sun and the largest planet in the solar system. NASA gives its radius as about 11 times Earth's; a hollow Jupiter-sized shell could contain roughly 1,000 Earths.

### A very short day

Jupiter rotates once in about 9.9 hours, the shortest day of any planet in the solar system. This rapid rotation helps make visible cloud bands race around the planet.

### Bands and the Great Red Spot

Jupiter's visible stripes are cloud belts and zones in an atmosphere made mainly of hydrogen and helium. The Great Red Spot is a giant long-lived storm currently larger than Earth. The page may animate cloud motion and a storm vortex, but it must not imply the visible colors are a solid surface.

### No Earth-like surface

Jupiter is a gas giant. It does not have a solid surface like Earth; pressure and temperature increase dramatically deeper inside.

## Scope guardrails

- Do not depict Jupiter as a rocky ball underneath a thin atmosphere.
- Do not use current moon counts; they are not needed for this entry and can change.
- Do not imply a child could stand on Jupiter's visible cloud tops.
- Keep the 1,000-Earth comparison explicitly volumetric/schematic rather than a claim that 1,000 Earths fit side-by-side across its diameter.
- Do not imply the Great Red Spot is permanent or unchanged; call it a long-lived storm.
