## Accepted claims

### Senses collect information; the brain interprets it

Eyes, ears, nose, mouth and sensory nerves in the skin/body collect different kinds of information. Nerves carry signals to the brain, where those signals are processed into experiences such as sights, sounds, smells, tastes and touch.

### Vision starts with light

Light enters the eye and is focused onto the retina. Light-sensitive cells in the retina convert light into electrical signals that travel through the optic nerve to the brain. Vision depends on both eyes and brain.

### Hearing starts with vibration

Sound waves make the eardrum and tiny middle-ear bones vibrate. The inner ear converts those vibrations into electrical signals that travel to the brain.

### Taste and smell work together strongly

Taste cells respond to at least five basic taste qualities: sweet, sour, salty, bitter and umami. These taste cells are distributed across the tongue rather than segregated into a simple "taste map." Smell contributes enormously to what we experience as flavor.

### Touch is broader than one feeling

Sensory nerves carry information such as touch, pressure, temperature and pain. Keep the child page focused on safe everyday textures/temperature and avoid pain demonstrations.

### People sense the world differently

People can have different sensory abilities, sensitivities and access needs. Do not frame one sensory profile as the only "normal" way to experience the world.

## Scope guardrails

- Do not present the familiar five senses as the complete scientific inventory of human sensory systems.
- Explicitly reject the false tongue taste-map diagram.
- Do not imply the eye or ear alone "understands" what is seen/heard; brain processing is essential.
- Do not use painful, startling, excessively loud or bright stimuli as demonstrations.
- Do not imply every person sees, hears, smells, tastes or feels in the same way.
- Keep accessibility language neutral and inclusive; sensory differences are not failure states.

## Approved narration wording

### `encyclopedia.body.senses.intro`

**it:** Il corpo raccoglie informazioni in tanti modi. Occhi, orecchie, naso, bocca e nervi della pelle inviano segnali al cervello, che li usa per costruire quello che vediamo, sentiamo e percepiamo.

**en:** Your body collects information in many ways. Eyes, ears, nose, mouth, and sensory nerves send signals to your brain, which uses them to build what you see, hear, and feel.

### `encyclopedia.body.senses.sight`

**it:** La luce entra nell'occhio e raggiunge la retina, sensibile alla luce. La retina trasforma la luce in segnali elettrici che viaggiano lungo il nervo ottico fino al cervello.

**en:** Light enters your eye and reaches the light-sensitive retina. The retina turns light into electrical signals that travel along the optic nerve to your brain.

### `encyclopedia.body.senses.hearing`

**it:** Le onde sonore fanno vibrare il timpano e minuscole ossa dell'orecchio. Nell'orecchio interno queste vibrazioni vengono trasformate in segnali che il nervo uditivo porta al cervello.

**en:** Sound waves make your eardrum and tiny ear bones vibrate. In the inner ear, those vibrations are turned into signals that the auditory nerve carries to your brain.

### `encyclopedia.body.senses.flavor`

**it:** Dolce, acido, salato, amaro e umami possono essere percepiti in molte zone della lingua: la famosa mappa della lingua è sbagliata. E quando mangi, gusto e olfatto lavorano molto insieme per creare il sapore.

**en:** Sweet, sour, salty, bitter, and umami can be sensed across many parts of the tongue; the famous tongue taste map is wrong. When you eat, taste and smell also work closely together to create flavor.

### `encyclopedia.body.senses.touch`

**it:** I nervi sensoriali portano al cervello informazioni come il tocco, la pressione e la temperatura. Persone diverse possono percepire il mondo in modi un po' diversi, ed è normale.

**en:** Sensory nerves carry information such as touch, pressure, and temperature to your brain. Different people can experience the world in somewhat different ways, and that is normal.
